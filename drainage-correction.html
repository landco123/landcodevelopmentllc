Match the Drainage Work to the Water Problem

Water moving across the surface, saturated soil, and a yard sloping toward the house can require different corrections. Some projects combine drainage and grading.

Surface Drains & Catch Basins

Collect water at low spots, runoff paths, or areas where water pools. Catch basins and solid drain pipe route collected surface water toward an appropriate outlet, subject to the site's available elevations.

Downspout Tie-Ins

Move roof water away from the area where a downspout empties beside the house. Landco evaluates pipe routing, discharge, and how the roof runoff affects the rest of the yard.

Negative Grade & Slope Correction

When ground slopes toward a structure, grading may be part of the correction. We evaluate fixed elevations, access, existing surfaces, and the available water path before reshaping the ground. See our grading services.

Swales, Berms & Runoff Routing

Shape surface water paths to intercept or guide runoff around problem areas. A swale, diversion berm, or dry creek bed may be appropriate where the site has sufficient fall and a suitable discharge location.

Washout Repair & Erosion Control

Repair eroded areas and address the concentrated runoff that caused the washout. Depending on the scope, stabilization may include seed, straw, erosion-control matting, or rock protection. Finish and stabilization work are identified in the written proposal.

French Drain Installation

A French drain can help collect water within saturated soil where site conditions and discharge permit. It has its own dedicated service page: French drain installation. Surface runoff may also need a catch basin or grading correction.

Plan the Outlet Before Installing Pipe

A drainage system needs somewhere for the water to go. Landco reviews the incoming water, low points, ground elevations, machine access, and proposed outlet. Gravity drainage depends on available fall; adding pipe alone does not correct every drainage problem.

  • Identify whether water comes from roof downspouts, a driveway, the surrounding slope, or a saturated area.
  • Evaluate whether surface collection, grading, underground drainage, or a combination fits the site.
  • Confirm the proposed route and outlet, including property boundaries, private underground systems, and any required approvals.
  • Define excavation, materials, finish grading, and stabilization in a written scope before work begins.

Fixed elevations and shallow private systems can limit routing. Irrigation, private utility lines, and other buried items need to be disclosed before excavation. The agreed scope sets the project limits and responsibilities.

Landco does not pour concrete or asphalt. Drainage or grading work beside an existing paved surface is evaluated as part of the site review.

Drainage Questions

Do I need a French drain or a surface drain?

Start with the source of the water. A surface drain collects runoff at the ground surface; a French drain collects water within the soil. Grading may be needed when the ground sends water toward the house. Landco evaluates the site before recommending a system.

Can you correct a yard that slopes toward the house?

Negative grade correction is part of Landco's grading and drainage work. What can be changed depends on elevations, access, existing improvements, and where water can discharge.

Can you repair erosion and washouts?

Yes. The scope should address both the eroded ground and the water path causing the damage. Stabilization is specified separately so you know whether seed, straw, matting, or other protection is included.

What should I send with a project request?

Send the project location, a description of what happens when it rains, and photos of the affected area and surrounding slope. Photos help qualify the project; elevations and an on-site review may still be needed to define the correction.
