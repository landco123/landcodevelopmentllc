What a Typical Land Clearing Scope Can Include

Every property is different. Some jobs need a simple brush clearing. Others need forestry mulching, fence line cleanup, debris handling, grading, drainage correction or site preparation before the property is ready for the next step.

Typical Work Items

  • Walk the site and identify access, vegetation, debris and next-phase needs
  • Clear brush, overgrowth, vines, briars, saplings and unwanted vegetation
  • Clean up fence lines, access paths, work areas and property edges
  • Use forestry mulching when appropriate for the site and finish goal
  • Plan debris handling: mulch, pile, haul-off or client-directed cleanup
  • Prepare the area for grading, drainage, driveways, pads, fencing or site preparation

Landco Standard

Clearing should not just knock down brush. It should leave the property cleaner, more accessible, easier to maintain and better prepared for grading, drainage, fencing, pads, driveways or the next phase of work.

That is the difference between basic clearing and development-minded site preparation.

Why Clearing Jobs Fail

  • No plan for what happens after the brush is removed
  • Access paths and fence lines left too rough to use
  • Stumps, debris or uneven ground block the next phase of work
  • Drainage and runoff problems get ignored during clearing
  • Property is cleared but not shaped, stabilized or prepared
  • Clearing, grading and drainage are treated as separate problems

Land Clearing Questions

A few common questions property owners ask before scheduling land clearing, brush clearing, forestry mulching or site preparation work.

Do you handle small land clearing jobs?

Yes. Landco Development LLC handles brush clearing, fence line clearing, access clearing, small lot clearing and clearing tied to grading, drainage or site preparation when the scope fits the equipment and property.

Do you provide grading after land clearing?

Yes. Many land clearing projects need grading afterward so the property is not just cleared, but also shaped into usable ground for driveways, pads, drainage, fencing or the next phase of work.

Do you haul away debris?

Debris handling depends on the job. Some projects involve mulching, piling, haul-off or client-directed cleanup. The right approach depends on access, site conditions, budget and final use.

Is forestry mulching always the best option?

Not always. Forestry mulching can be efficient for brush, saplings and overgrowth, but some properties also need grading, debris handling, access correction, drainage work or site preparation.

Can clearing help prepare for fencing?

Yes. Fence line clearing can clean up property lines, access lanes and boundary areas before fence installation, fence repair, livestock areas or regular maintenance.

What should I send for a land clearing quote?

Send the project location, project type, photos or video if available, what needs to be cleared, whether grading or drainage is needed afterward and whether debris should be mulched, piled, hauled off or handled another way.
